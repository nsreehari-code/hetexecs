Stage 3: Design

Curriculum architecture (not content yet), assessment philosophy, evidence plan. Structure before slides.

Core Question

"What is essential vs nice-to-have? What must be practiced vs explained? What evidence can be produced during learning?"

What You Decide (Before Making Content)

  • Module structure — what's essential vs nice-to-have
  • Practice-heavy activities — what learners do, not just hear
  • Assessment approach — how competency is proven
  • Evidence plan — what artifacts the learner produces at each stage
  • Trainer prerequisites — who can teach this
  • Local language considerations

This Stage Produces

  • Modular curriculum architecture
  • Assessable moments (not just a final exam)
  • Drop-in adaptability (can swap funding model without redesign)
  • Assessment rubrics
  • Evidence capture checklist

What You Create

  • CCF Sections A–C complete (Identity + Outcome + QF — refinement pass)
  • Trainer guide outline
  • Learner workbook outline
  • Assessment rubrics (draft)
  • Evidence capture plan

Actors

ActorRole
CEOArchitecture sign-off, evidence philosophy
Curriculum designer / SMEModule design, assessment rubrics, workbook
Trainer (experienced)Feasibility check — "can this actually be taught in 8 weeks to this audience?"

Funding Adapter Differences

ModelWhat changes
GovtAssessment must be third-party + portal recorded

PaidAssessment optional but increases perceived value

Handoff to Execution

Once Stage 3 is complete, the course is architecturally ready. The CCF (sections A–C) becomes the strategic anchor. Sections D–I are filled during execution (hetops).

The next stage — Stage 4: Package — is owned by hetops.
